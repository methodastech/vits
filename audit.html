Vit's Noodles · vitsnoodles.com · Web Audit

Fifty one years of capability, invisible to the buyer who matters.

Every figure below was measured live on vitsnoodles.com between 10 and 12 August 2026, not estimated. The finding in one line: the site is a working content factory, 127 URLs deep, with real trade credentials, wearing a shop window that publishes none of the numbers a trade buyer needs and loads slower than a buyer will wait.

127 live URLs in the sitemap 7.0s homepage load 0 MOQ, capacity or lead-time figures published 241 of 253 homepage images without alt text 4 spellings of the brand name on one page 3.4 / 10 nine-lens score
The 60 second verdict

vitsnoodles.com is not a brochure site and not a rescue case. It is WordPress with Yoast and WooCommerce, the SEO fundamentals are present, and the recipe and blog engines are genuinely active, the most recent recipe update landed 6 August. What it lacks is a lane for the buyer Vit's now says matters most: the trade buyer at Gulfood, in the US, Bangkok and the UK. That buyer finds no MOQ, no production capacity, no lead time, a 7 second load, and a brand name spelled four ways. The fix is not a teardown. It is a fast trade destination in front, a disciplined rebuild behind it, and a 127 URL migration that preserves every ranking, which is the client's own stated fear.

01
The company before the site
Who Vit's actually is, and the six jobs a winning trade site must do for them

An audit that starts with the site judges aesthetics. This one starts with the company, so every score that follows is a score against revenue. Vit's Noodles, VIT Makanan (K.L.) Sdn. Bhd., has manufactured noodles in Kuala Lumpur since 1975, halal certified by JAKIM since 1980.

The commercial model

Plain noodles pay for everything

Roughly 70% of revenue is the Malaysian plain-noodle base, led by the yellow economy pack. Seasoned noodles, premium ranges and the Italian line sit on top. The base cannot be disturbed, the top is where growth lives.

The growth engine

OEM, private label, bulk

Behind the retail brand is a manufacturing business: OEM runs, private label for overseas buyers, bulk supply. Its customers are found at exhibitions, Dubai first, then the US, Bangkok and the UK, and its orders are containers, not cartons.

The catalogue

25 SKUs across 7 categories

Economy, premium Penang range, air-dried Healthy Series, Hotlicious pan mee, Italian series, fresh lines. Fried and air-dried processes, certifications FSSC 22000, HACCP, GMP, ISO 22000, JAKIM halal.

Six things a winning Vit's site has to do

Job 1

Answer the buyer's opening question, can you make a taste like Buldak, like Indomie, before the first email.

Job 2

Publish the three trade numbers: MOQ tiers, capacity, lead time, plus formats and shelf life per SKU.

Job 3

Serve two lanes without confusing either: the 70% Malaysian consumer base and the overseas trade buyer.

Job 4

Load inside a buyer's patience on a phone at an exhibition stand, under 3 seconds to first meaningful paint.

Job 5

Say every fact once, correctly: one brand spelling, one export count, one founding claim, all evidenced.

Job 6

Keep the rankings it already owns: 127 URLs of recipes, posts and products migrate with zero 404s.

The rest of this audit scores the site against these six jobs. Every chapter names the job it serves.

03
What a trade buyer finds today
The site graded against the five things an OEM buyer checks before emailing

The 12 August meeting reset the brief: the priority customer is the trade buyer at exhibitions, importers, distributors, wholesalers and private label clients. Published buyer research and trade platform practice agree on what that buyer evaluates first: packaging formats, shelf life, certifications, minimum order quantity, and customisation ability. Vit's publishes one of the five well.

F2 · The five checks an OEM buyer runs, vs what vitsnoodles.com publishes, read 12 Aug 2026
The buyer's opening question

"Can you create a taste similar to Buldak, or Indomie?"

Christina's words for how buyers open. The site's answer today is silence: seasoned noodle capability is the thing Vit's says nobody knows about them, and the site never demonstrates it. The seasoning story is a content job before it is a branding job.

The export claim conflict

One fact, four numbers

The About page says 30 countries, the OEM page says 30+, a blog post says 20, and the client says 50+ with 30 years of export experience. A buyer who notices stops trusting every other number. The real figure is owed by Christina, then published once, everywhere.

Trade platform practice

Tiered MOQ converts better than silence

Traditional OEM minimums in this category run 10,000+ units per SKU, and listings that publish tiered MOQs convert materially better than single-figure or absent MOQs. Publishing the number is not a concession, it is a filter that saves the sales team from unqualified enquiries.

The single highest-value fix

Publish MOQ, capacity, lead time

It needs no rebrand, no rebuild and no designer. Three figures from operations, published on the trade pages, would do more for exhibition follow-up than any visual change on this site. Owed by Vit's operations, chased through Christina.

04
The shop window
The homepage, measured

The homepage tries to serve two audiences in one carousel and serves neither. Slide one sells OEM manufacturing to businesses, slide two sells "Loved Worldwide" to consumers. Below the fold it carries 483 words, thin for a site with this much to say, and the word count problem is small next to the naming problem.

Measured 11 Aug

The brand name, four ways on one page

On the Private Label page the name appears 19 times in 4 different spellings, and the most used form accounts for only 47% of mentions. Across the site: the title tag says "Vit's Noodle" singular, the meta says "Vits Noodles", Facebook is /vitsnoodles, Instagram is @vits_malaysia, and the registered entity is VIT Makanan (K.L.) Sdn Bhd. Both apostrophe characters appear.

Why it matters

This is the brand book argument, in evidence

Vit's has never had brand guidelines, Christina confirmed it outright in the 12 August meeting. The spelling table is what 51 years without a rule book looks like in public. It is also the one audit finding that no competitor can copy their way out of for us: it is fixed by governance, which is what we sell.

Date claims

1975, 1977, 1980, pick one

The site trades on "since 1975". One blog title tag reads "A Malaysian Noodle Brand Since 1980". Company histories place the first noodle machine at 1977. The safe, sourced line is "halal since 1980", JAKIM, which is also the more valuable claim for the Gulf buyer. "Making the noodle since 1975" needs the client's evidence before it goes on a pack or a page.

Heard in the meeting

The curry correction

Our July line "curry appears zero times on the homepage" was heard by Christina as "you have no curry", and she pushed back, correctly, that curry SKUs exist under the Classic range. The finding stands in its precise form: the homepage does not surface the word. The framing changes: curry is a visibility gap, not a product gap, and its job is now the overseas seasoning story, not a Malaysian launch.

F5 · The brand name on one page, counted, OEM page, 28 Jul 2026
Claims discipline, standing rule for every deliverable: no export count, no "no preservatives", and no founding-year claim goes on the new site until Vit's evidences it. "Halal since 1980" is sourced and stays.
05
Technical SEO
What Yoast already does, and the three schema gaps worth real traffic

Credit first: the fundamentals are present. Every page checked carries a title, a meta description at page level, a single H1, a canonical, and Yoast's WebPage, WebSite and Breadcrumb schema. Blog posts carry Article schema. This is a 6 out of 10 technical base, not a disaster. The gaps are specific, and the largest one is sitting on the site's most active content.

06
Performance
Measured against the only clock that matters, a buyer's patience

Re-measured 10 August: the homepage delivers 3.41MB over 114 requests and completes in 7.0 seconds, worse than the 6.4 seconds measured on 28 July. Christina's stated fear, from her previous agency, is that animation makes sites heavy. The truth on her own site is the opposite: the weight is images and plugin payload, not motion. There is no animation on vitsnoodles.com, and it is still slow.

07
The content engines
The two machines already running, and the shop window that hides them

Two genuinely valuable engines run on this site. The recipe hub, 44 recipes and 11 videos, freshest content on the domain, is a B2C retention machine. The blog, 24 posts on OEM, private label and export, is a B2B education machine, exactly the material a trade buyer reads. Neither is surfaced from the homepage in any structured way, neither carries its schema type, and the recipes have no filtering by range or heat.

B2C engine

44 recipes, 11 videos

Updated 6 August. With Recipe schema and a filterable hub this becomes the organic front door for the consumer lane, and the natural home of the heat-ladder story.

B2B engine

24 posts on trade topics

Last post 9 July. Migrated intact and cross-linked from the trade destination, this is the evidence base for the seasoning capability story buyers ask about.

Commerce layer

25 products, 7 categories

WooCommerce is installed and structured, but products carry no gram weights on 7 of 8 SKUs checked, no meta descriptions and no Product schema. E-commerce expansion is in the client's own pipeline, the plumbing already exists.

08
The two-lane resolution
How one site serves a 70% Malaysian B2C business and an overseas B2B push without confusing either

Christina's constraint is explicit: 70% of revenue is Malaysian and the site "cannot be too B2B". The buyer she wants is overseas trade. Brian's proposal in the meeting, which she confirmed aligns with their own pipeline, is separation: the Malaysian B2C site stays and improves, and a dedicated trade destination carries the export story on its own subdomain, in front of exhibition traffic, with zero risk to existing rankings.
