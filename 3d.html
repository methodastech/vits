The measurements

How wide, and how we know

The width was asked for twice and argued about three times, so here is the whole working. There are no published dimensions for this pack. The manufacturer’s own listing gives 350g, twelve packs to a carton and thirteen months of shelf life, and no measurements; the retailers, the halal directory and the export listings give none either. So the photographs are the only authority, and they do not agree with each other.

Why they disagree, and which one won

A bag photographed lying on a floor splays its gussets open, which widens it. That explains part of the gap between the retail photograph and the studio plate, but not all of it. The two face-on references, which are the view a shopper takes and the view this site’s hero takes, both say the pack is nearly as wide as it is tall. The three-quarter studio plate says it is a third narrower.

The build follows the face-on references, at 0.93 of its height wide. Face-on it reads 1.02 tall per wide, between the retail bag’s 0.961 and the client’s own 1.062. What that costs is the three-quarter silhouette, which boxes 0.99 against that plate’s 1.131.

What it does not cost is the match itself. The front scored 69.5% at 0.93 and 69.3% at 0.78, because the print is drawn for the width it is given: the pre-stretch is derived from the width, so round things stay round at any width. Going back to the plate-derived 0.78 is one number in the source, and the reasoning sits beside it.

Side by side

The render, against the pack

Same view, same height, no retouching: the model on the left, the client’s own photograph on the right. Every number is measured, not judged. Silhouette is how much of the two outlines overlap. Print placement is how well the printed ink lands in the same places. Colour is how close the two are over the pack itself.

Piece by piece

Every graphic, on its own

Each element is cut out of the flat artwork and out of the client’s photograph on the same frame, so it can be judged without the rest of the pack around it. Three of these were found wrong this way and are fixed below.

The mark

It is FLAT: 1.82 wide per tall, and it was drawn at 1.38. Two readings agree, ref-05 gives 1.85 and the studio plate 1.79 once the view is taken out. The printed ellipse is flatter than a first pass drew it, and the wider face the bag now has is what let it sit right.

The phoenix

Was reading larger than the printed bird. 200 to 172, and lifted 8px.

The border

Wrong twice. A stroked path with round joins read as brackets; flat bars on a rail merged the row into one band. The pack has self-contained square spirals with clear yellow between them.

The noodle oval

Dropped and pulled in so it crowds neither the mark above it nor the count beside it, which is how the pack has it.

The count block

Moved 29px off the fold. Measured on the plate it starts at 0.045 of the face; it was drawing 0.017, hard against the crease.

The halal chop

The extracted artwork had lost the final L and read HALA. Re-cut from the retail photograph: MALAYSIA around the top, the eight-point star, the Arabic, HALAL beneath.

The wave column

Ten strands at 0.0359 of the face, running border to border. It was drawing eight at a coarser pitch and stopping short of the fold.

The net weight

Set hard against the right edge over the wave column, as printed.

The record

Every fix, and what found it

Each of these came out of a measurement against the reference wall, not out of an opinion. They are in the order they happened.

  1. 30 Aug
    Started again from nothing

    The production pack was a box with a bulge. V2 begins as one lofted shape in bare white film, judged on silhouette alone before any artwork goes near it.

  2. 2 Sep
    A pillow, not a carton

    Beside the photograph the bag read as a box: knife-edge folds, a flat base, a flat front. The corners now carry a real radius, the base rounds under as the plate’s does, and the front pillows enough to catch the softbox. Width stays at Bazil’s 0.93: the three-quarter photograph only looks thinner because of its angle.

  3. 2 Sep
    The back, with nothing on top of anything

    The phoenix and mark straddled the top border and the barcode printed through the bottom one. The mark sits below the band now, the left column measures itself and squeezes to the space it has, and the red panel ends above the border. Audited: zero ink inside either border band.

  4. 2 Sep
    The real cake in both windows

    The front oval carries the client’s own window photograph, print and all; the gussets carry the cake at the plate’s strand density under the mark and type. The drawn curls and the magnified stock strands are gone from v2.

  5. 2 Sep
    Lit like the studio

    The room probe lit the film from every side, so the roof washed to (254,203,129) and the flat front carried 46 of blue against the plate’s 2. Now a dark room, one softbox front-right, a grey wall for the gussets to reflect and a warm side fill. Field, gusset and solid red each land within a few levels of the photograph.

  6. 2 Sep
    The plate’s camera

    Every front element sat higher than the photograph and the roof read short. One elevated camera explained all of it: the studio shot is 8 degrees above eye level. The v2 view takes that camera, and the print rows fell into place.

  7. 2 Sep
    The seal fin, half the height

    Measured on the plate the red band runs rows 2 to 6 of the box with a yellow film margin above it; it was drawn 7.6% of the height and read as a slab. Now 6.4% with the script on the band, and its ends droop.

  8. 2 Sep
    Every element re-measured on the standing bag

    Halal chop, mark, window oval, ribbon, count block, net weight, both borders and the gusset’s mark and three-size type were each cut out of studio-3q and the render on the same frame and moved until the boxes agree. The window oval was 21% too tall and sat left of the mark; the count cell was short; the gusset type was set half size.

  9. 2 Sep
    Wider, on Bazil’s call

    The two studio plates solve to 0.76 to 0.80 wide per tall; the retail bag in hand reads near square. Bazil asked for wider twice, so the bag stands at 0.93 of its height, 1.05 tall per wide front-on, and every print element is drawn for that width.

  10. 1 Sep
    Shape measured, not eyeballed

    The studio plate is thresholded into a mask and the render is matched to it row by row. Aspect, seal width, shoulder height and the ear position all moved.

  11. 1 Sep
    Two plates cross-solve the box

    One view cannot separate depth from angle. Solving the front and the gusset plates together put the bag at 0.73 deep per wide. Every version before this was a slab.

  12. 1 Sep
    The roof folds instead of lofting

    Every point on the roof runs in a straight line from the crease to the seal, so the panels come out flat and the gusset corners fold into real dog ears.

  13. 1 Sep
    The print goes on, in four panels

    Each panel owns its own copy of the creases it sits between, so it gets its own texture coordinates and its own normals. A real fold down each corner.

  14. 1 Sep
    The border was half size

    ref-05 is a crop, not the full face, and the pitch was taken off the inset rather than the band. The module repeats 23 times across the face on the pack; it was drawing 46.

  15. 1 Sep
    The seal is yellow with red script

    Sampled inside the band on both plates: yellow is 61% and 65% of the pixels. An earlier round had drawn a red band with yellow script.

  16. 1 Sep
    The view angle was wrong by 8 degrees

    A scan put the front plate’s fold at x=158. Enlarged, it is at 207. That one number sets the whole box, and it had the print rendering 10% oversized.

  17. 1 Sep
    The two gussets are a mirrored pair

    The mark sits against the front edge on both panels, so their layouts mirror. One texture on both had put the mark against the back seam on the right-hand side.

  18. 1 Sep
    Width set against the photographs

    Measured height over width: 1.062 on the marketing render, 1.016 on the retail bag, 1.135 on the studio plate. Two passes overshot before it landed at 1.048.

  19. 1 Sep
    The claim is a ribbon

    Enlarged on the plate the ends are cut with a V and a darker fold sits behind each tail. It was a plain rectangle.

  20. 1 Sep
    The bottom border comes down

    Read off a tenths grid on the gusset plate, the border sits lower than it was drawn. The two plates disagree by 7%, so it sits at the measured middle.

  21. 1 Sep
    Six corrections off Bazil’s eye

    The seal is red with yellow script, not the reverse: a pixel count inside the band said otherwise and it was counting the roof’s own yellow. The mark comes down 10%, the corner creases sharpen, the count block moves off the fold to where the plate has it, the noodle oval drops so it crowds neither the mark nor the count, and the claim becomes a ribbon with hanging tails.

  22. 1 Sep
    The back panel, rebuilt off the photograph

    Three independent readings of the client’s back shot agreed on a layout the render did not have: a red panel with a wavy edge down one side carrying the nutrition table and the allergen block, two bilingual sections set in BLACK ink rather than red, an Arabic pair, a certification strip with HACCP and the origin lines, and the barcode beside the address. What was there before was four centred red-headed paragraphs.

  23. 1 Sep
    A checklist that runs itself

    Every accuracy claim is now a measurement with a stated target, run against the exported artwork and the render. It caught the count block sitting one pixel off the noodle oval, which is a collision and not a gap, and it caught two bugs in its own measurements before any of it was trusted.

  24. 1 Sep
    A review found what the checklist missed

    Eleven checks passed while the front's bottom border was broken in three places, the gusset window printed through both of its borders, the claim ribbon punched holes in the film, the back's address printed through the Arabic, and the two gusset plates wore a different mark from the front. All fixed, and the checklist grew eight checks that would have caught them.

  25. 1 Sep
    Every front element laid to the plate, and a ribbon that is a ribbon

    The phoenix, mark, count block, halal chop and noodle oval were each measured on the studio plate as a fraction of the face width and of the wall height, and the same fractions put them on the canvas. The mark is drawn at 2.18 so that after the panel mapping's 1.2x vertical stretch it renders at the plate's 1.8. The count cell comes down from 139px to the pack's 84. The claim is rebuilt the way a banner hangs: a sagging band between two folds, with the swallowtails on the tails that hang below, not cut into the band.

  26. 2 Sep
    Element by element against the floor photo: the mark

    The mark’s white ring was torn along its lower arc and 唯一麵 sat 0.15 of the half-width too high. The scan’s own glyphs are kept and moved, the ring is stroked clean at the printed inset. Along the way: a pixel read that ignored the canvas transform, and a throw inside the gusset’s rotation that had been silently aborting every repaint.

  27. 3 Sep
    The shape came first, and the width with it

    Every print measurement was standing on the silhouette, so the silhouette was solved before anything else. At 0.93 wide the render boxed 1.004 tall per wide against the studio plate’s 1.131, which no camera can reach: a bag’s projected width can never fall below its own width. The section went from n=7 to n=12, because the plate’s vertical folds are creases and not pillow rounds, and the width to 0.78. The box now reads 1.130 and the roof profile matches the plate row for row from the seal to the base.

  28. 3 Sep
    Eight elements, one at a time

    On the solved frame each printed element was cut from the plate and from the render into the same window and read side by side. The count block lost its oversized Mini and got the 5 in Arial Black at three quarters of its cell. The meander bands came down from 63px to 50 on a 0.887 pitch. The halal chop went round, at 0.985 aspect, where it had been printing an oval. The phoenix moved onto the plate’s own axis. Overlap on the front went from 40.5% to 49.5%.

  29. 3 Sep
    The banner reads as a ribbon

    It was a red bar with a small label in the middle. The plate’s banner carries its line across nearly the whole band, and the type is now stretched to fit it, with swallow tails at both ends. A first attempt ran it across the crease onto the gusset, on a row scan that had caught the sealed fin above it; the pair image put it back at 0.30 to 0.84 of the box, which is where the pack prints it.

  30. 3 Sep
    Two fixes measured, then put back

    The base wants a harder fold: the plate is 0.68 of the box wide at 94% down where the render is 0.81. Taking the fold further up the wall shortened the bag itself, the box fell to 1.090 against the plate’s 1.131, and the front lost six points. The softer curve stays, with the reason written beside it in the source. Lifting the gusset lockup by the full 3.3% the judge asked for cost the left panel five points, so it moved half that.

  31. 3 Sep
    The gusset, against the side-on photograph

    The three-quarter view foreshortens this panel to nothing, so the gusset was judged against the straight-on plate instead. The window was 0.76 of the panel wide against the photograph’s 0.58, which is why moving it had done nothing: the ellipse simply clipped at both edges. Narrowed to 0.62, the roundel taken out to the plate’s width and the three lines up 12%, the gusset went from 57.8% to 60.0% on the right and 64.4% to 65.1% on the left.

  32. 3 Sep
    No published dimensions exist for this pack

    Asked to check the size online: the manufacturer’s own listing gives 350g, twelve packs to a carton and thirteen months, and no dimensions. Nor do the retailers, the halal directory or the export listings. The nearest published figure is a competitor’s five-pack at 17 x 12.5 x 9 cm, which is a different weight and a different bundle. So the two studio photographs stay the authority, and the model now agrees with both of them: 1.130 tall per wide against the three-quarter’s 1.131, and 0.627 wide per tall side-on against the gusset plate’s 0.632.

  33. 3 Sep
    Wider, and this time with the working shown

    Bazil, looking at the hero face-on: too pressed together. He is right, and the two face-on references agree with him: the retail bag reads 0.961 tall per wide and the client’s own render 1.062, where the studio three-quarter implies a third narrower. The bag went to 0.93 of its height wide, which reads 1.02 face-on. The print was re-fitted to the new width straight afterwards, so nothing drifted: the phoenix, the halal chop and the count block moved back onto the plate by their measured offsets, and the banner widened with the roof. Front match 69.5% to 70.0% before the banner, 69.7% after. The whole argument is set out in the measurements section above.

  34. 3 Sep
    A better face, and a shorter menu

    The display face was Archivo condensed at 800, a competent grotesk that read like every other food site. It is now Fraunces, a variable serif with real optical sizing and a WONK axis, set at 900 and optical size 144: a heritage masthead for a brand that has been on Malaysian shelves since 1975. The body face went from Plus Jakarta Sans to Instrument Sans. Fraunces sets wider than a condensed face, so the hero and the reel headline came down a step to fit, and the rest of the page was checked element by element for overflow. About, Products and Resources came out of the menu on every page.
